## Topic 1: Music

**Q: What kind of music do you listen to?**
My taste is fairly broad, but I'd say I gravitate towards indie rock and a bit of jazz, especially when I'm working. What I particularly enjoy about jazz is that it's never quite the same twice, so even an album I've listened to a hundred times can still surprise me. I've started going to small live gigs in town as well, which I find much more engaging than listening to recorded music.

**Q: Do you prefer listening to music alone or with others?**
It depends on the mood, really. When I'm working or need to concentrate, I prefer listening alone because I can get completely absorbed in it. But there's something special about sharing music with friends—whether that's discussing a new album or attending a concert together. I think music becomes even more meaningful when you can share your favourite tracks with people whose taste you respect.

**Q: Have you ever been to a live concert?**
Yes, several times actually. The most memorable was a small indie band concert in a venue that held maybe a hundred people. The atmosphere was incredibly intimate, and the lead singer actually came down from the stage to chat with the audience between songs. There's a completely different energy at live events compared to listening at home.

**Q: Do you think music education is important for children?**
Absolutely. Beyond the obvious benefits for creativity and expression, learning music teaches discipline and patience. Mastering an instrument requires countless hours of practice, which builds resilience. I also think it helps children develop their listening skills and an appreciation for craftsmanship in general.

## Topic 2: The Area You Live In

**Q: Tell me about the area where you live.**
I live in a fairly central neighbourhood that's undergone significant development over the past decade. There's a nice balance between residential buildings and commercial spaces. What I appreciate most is the sense of community—people tend to know their neighbours, and there's a weekly market that brings the area to life every Saturday morning.

**Q: Do you think your neighbourhood has changed much over the years?**
Definitely. When I first moved here, there were far fewer cafes and restaurants. Now the street corners are dotted with independent coffee shops and eateries. The flip side is that property prices have skyrocketed, and some of the older families have moved away because they can no longer afford the area. It's become somewhat gentrified.

**Q: What do you like most about your neighbourhood?**
The convenience, without a doubt. Everything I need on a daily basis is within walking distance—from the supermarket to the gym to my favourite bookshop. I barely use my car during the week. There's also a lovely park nearby where I often go for a run or just to unwind.

**Q: Would you recommend this area to visitors?**
I would, yes, particularly if they're interested in exploring local culture rather than tourist traps. The area has retained much of its character despite the development. There's a particular street with beautiful old architecture that I always point out to friends visiting from out of town.

## Topic 3: Reading

**Q: Do you enjoy reading? What kind of books do you read?**
Yes, I'm quite an avid reader, though I have to admit my reading habits have changed quite a bit over the years. I used to read mostly fiction, but these days I gravitate towards non-fiction—particularly history and popular science. I'm currently working through a biography of a historical figure, which combines my interest in both narrative storytelling and factual learning.

**Q: Do you prefer physical books or e-books?**
I go back and forth on this. For fiction and books I want to savour, I definitely prefer physical copies. There's something about the weight of a book, the smell of the pages, and the satisfaction of turning actual pages that e-readers can't quite replicate. However, for travel or when I'm reading technical material where I need to search for specific information, e-books are incredibly practical.

**Q: How often do you read?**
I'd say I read for at least thirty minutes to an hour each day, usually before bed. Weekends I can sometimes lose myself in a book for entire afternoons. I find it a much more restful way to unwind compared to scrolling through my phone, which tends to leave my mind buzzing.

**Q: Do you think reading is important for children?**
Enormously. Reading not only expands vocabulary and comprehension skills, but it also builds empathy by allowing children to experience the world through different perspectives. I think it sparks imagination in ways that visual media sometimes doesn't. Encouraging a habit of reading early on sets a foundation for lifelong learning.
